Nestled deep in the heart of the New Jersey Pine Barrens along the winding Wading River, Wading Pines Camping Resort (located at 85 Godfrey Bridge Rd in Chatsworth, NJ) offers a classic South Jersey outdoor escape surrounded by towering pines and sandy riverbanks. But towing a motorhome or large travel trailer through the twisting two-lane back roads of the Pinelands requires careful route planning.

Here is our verified ground-level breakdown to help you navigate approach roads, tree canopy clearance, pull-through pad layouts, and toad unhooking strategies.

🚚 The Quick Rig-Fit Scorecard

  • Recommended Max Rig Length: Up to 45 ft (select oversized pull-throughs accommodate up to 60 ft)
  • Toad / Towed Vehicle Friendliness: High (spacious registration area and pull-through staging room)
  • Canopy & Clearance Warning: Moderate overhead pine branches along interior sandy lanes
  • Best Sites for Big Rigs: Dedicated 50-amp pull-through sites near the main entrance/office
  • Atmosphere: Wooded, active, and family-oriented with river canoeing and tubing access

🛣️ Navigation & Pine Barrens Approach Route

Standard GPS routing through the Pinelands can occasionally try to push large motorhomes down narrow, unpaved sand tracks or low-clearance forest fire cuts. Stick to primary state and county arteries:

  • Approach via major regional connectors like Route 72 or Route 206 depending on your direction.
  • Navigate toward Chatsworth and access Godfrey Bridge Road via established local paved connecting roads to ensure smooth turning radiuses into the resort entrance.
⚠️ Sandy Shoulder & Tree Clearance Warning: Interior resort roads and back-in loops feature tight turns framed by mature pitch pines and sandy shoulders. Keep your eyes up for low-hanging branches that can clip roof air conditioners, satellite domes, or side awnings.

🚗 Toad Unhook Strategy

While the main office area is available, unhooking up front can be tight and stressful during peak weekend check-in rushes. Instead, plan your arrival around these two higher-utility staging strategies:

  • Unhook at Your Site: Many of the main pull-through sites provide enough straight-line length to pull your motorhome and toad fully into the pad, giving you a flat, out-of-traffic spot to disconnect your tow bar or dolly at your leisure.
  • Use the Rear Overflow Lot: If your assigned spot is a tight back-in loop, bypass the congested front gate and pull through toward the overflow parking lot located in the back corner of the campground. This area provides plenty of wide turning room to drop your towed vehicle before maneuvering into your campsite pad.

🏕️ Campground Layout & Big-Rig Realities

Pull-Throughs vs. Wooded Back-Ins

Wading Pines offers roughly 300 sites split between shaded rustic back-ins and full-hookup pull-throughs. If you are operating a heavy Class A or long fifth wheel, requesting a 50-amp pull-through site will save you from tricky backing angles between close pine trees.

Utilities & Hookup Notes

Most full-hookup sites feature reliable 30/50 amp electrical service, water, and sewer connections. Because the soil is sandy Pine Barrens terrain, leveling blocks are recommended to keep your rig stable on soft outer pad edges.
