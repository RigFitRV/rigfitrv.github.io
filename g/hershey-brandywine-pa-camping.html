🍫 Why Hershey & Brandywine is an Elite Central PA RV Destination

World-class theme parks, historic chocolate heritage, rolling agricultural landscapes, and proximity to the Susquehanna River make Hershey and Central Pennsylvania a premier RV destination. However, navigating heavy resort traffic, historical township rail bridges, and seasonal farm corridors requires clear arterial routing strategies for motorhomes and fifth wheels.

🚚 Rig Clearance & Heavy Highway Routing Guidance

When approaching the Hershey/Harrisburg region from the Pennsylvania Turnpike (I-76), I-83, US-322, or PA-283, big rig operators must remain aware of residential low-clearance shortcuts and tight township intersections.

Key Highway Approaches

  • PA Route 283 Corridor: The primary high-speed arterial connecting Harrisburg and Lancaster. Utilize direct exits for PA-741 and Turnpike Road for wide, unobstructed resort access.
  • US Route 322 (Hershey Bypass): Connects I-83 directly to Hershey attractions. Stick to major interchange exits (Hockersville Road / Sweet Street) to bypass town-center congestion.
  • PA Turnpike (I-76) Access: Utilize Exit 266 (Lebanon/Lancaster) to transition onto PA-72 or PA-741, maintaining central lane positioning along major agricultural corridors.
Pro Clearance Tip: Avoid cutting through minor township backroads near Mount Gretna or historic village shortcuts. Always remain on primary state-numbered arteries (US-322, PA-283, PA-741) until your final campground turnoff.

⚡ Local Infrastructure & Utility Insights

  • Power Grid Loads: Peak summer temperatures combined with full-capacity resort surges across Hershey properties can cause shore-power voltage drops. A heavy-duty surge protector with EMS protection is essential.
  • Water Pressure & Filtration: Central PA limestone ground aquifers and municipal connections feature varying mineral hardness; use an inline filter and set your pressure regulator to 45–50 PSI.
  • Cellular & Wi-Fi Coverage: Excellent 5G and 4G LTE signal availability across major providers (Verizon, AT&T, T-Mobile) along the US-322 and PA-283 corridors, with minor dips deep in wooded South Mountain hollows.
