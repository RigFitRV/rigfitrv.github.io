🌲 Why Maryland's Chesapeake Bay is an Elite RV Destination

From the confluence of the Potomac River at Point Lookout down to the saltmarshes of Tangier Sound at Janes Island, Maryland's Chesapeake watershed offers unmatched waterfront camping. Navigating the Eastern Shore and lower peninsulas requires careful attention to two-lane rural state highways, low-clearance causeways, and coastal wind exposure.

🚚 Rig Clearance & Heavy Highway Routing Guidance

Approaching coastal state parks and private waterfront resorts in Maryland involves transitioning from major dual-carriageway routes (like US-50 and US-13) onto winding secondary peninsular roads (MD-2, MD-213, MD-272, and MD-413).

Key Highway Approaches

  • Eastern Shore Access (US-50 / US-301): Wide, smooth divided highways with generous shoulders, ideal for big rigs and motorhomes towing vehicles.
  • Peninsular Secondary Routes (MD-213, MD-272, MD-413): Generally well-paved two-lane roads featuring rolling terrain and tightening tree canopies as you approach park boundaries.
Pro Clearance Tip: Watch for sudden coastal crosswinds near bridge causeways (such as approaches to Point Lookout and the lower Eastern Shore) and unhook towed vehicles in designated registration turnouts before entering tighter interior loops.

⚡ Local Infrastructure & Utility Insights

  • Power Grid Loads: Coastal state park and private campground pedestals experience peak electrical draws during hot summer weekends; a heavy-duty surge protector is mandatory.
  • Water Pressure & Filtration: Well and municipal water systems across the shore vary in pressure and mineral content; an adjustable pressure regulator with gauge is essential.
  • Cellular Coverage: Reliable LTE/5G coverage along major highway corridors, with occasional signal dips inside deep saltmarsh state parks like Janes Island.
