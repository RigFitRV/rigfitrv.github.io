🌲 Why the Hudson Valley & Catskills is an Elite RV Destination

From the dramatic cliffs of the Shawangunk Ridge to the deep mountain valleys surrounding the Catskill Forest Preserve, southeastern New York offers extraordinary natural beauty combined with rich historic riverfront towns. However, navigating this region in a motorhome or travel trailer requires strict attention to route selection. Historic stone arch bridges, narrow state park access corridors, and low-clearance parkways present real hazards for unprepared RV drivers.

🚚 Rig Clearance & Heavy Highway Routing Guidance

When traveling through the Hudson Valley and Catskills, maintaining commercial truck routes is non-negotiable. All New York State Parkways (including the Taconic State Parkway and Palisades Interstate Parkway) strictly prohibit commercial vehicles and towed trailers due to extremely low arched stone bridges.

Key Highway Approaches

  • Interstate 87 (NY State Thruway): The primary commercial corridor running north-south through the Hudson Valley. Use Exit 18 (New Paltz) for Gardiner, Exit 19 (Kingston) for Central Catskills connectors, and Exit 20 (Saugerties) for Northern Ulster County parks.
  • NY Route 17 / I-86 Corridor: The primary southern Catskills artery providing wide state highway access to Livingston Manor, Roscoe, and Sullivan County parks.
  • NY Route 32 & Route 28: Key commercial-approved state connectors offering wide lane margins and reliable overhead clearances into the interior mountain valleys.
Pro Clearance Tip: Never rely on standard auto GPS apps in Ulster or Sullivan County. Always verify routes against designated state truck corridors to avoid non-truck parkways and low rural railroad trestles.

⚡ Local Infrastructure & Utility Insights

  • Power Grid Loads: High summer occupancy in valley resorts can cause electrical line voltage sags on 30-amp loops. Surge protectors with low-voltage auto-cutoff are highly recommended.
  • Water Pressure & Filtration: Mountain and riverfront well systems often carry mineral deposits and high static pressure. Utilize dual-stage sediment filtration and a brass water pressure regulator set to 45–50 PSI.
  • Cellular Coverage: While highway corridors feature strong 5G connectivity, deep mountain hollows (such as Mongaup Pond) feature dead zones where satellite or offline map caching is required.
