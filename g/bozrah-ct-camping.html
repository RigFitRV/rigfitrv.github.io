🌲 Why Bozrah & Southeastern CT is an RV Destination

Nestled in the rolling hills of the Thames River Valley between Hartford and Providence, Bozrah, Connecticut serves as the premier base camp for RVers exploring Southeastern Connecticut. While many travelers head straight to coastal corridors, experienced RV drivers know that Bozrah and neighboring towns like Preston, Griswold, and Baltic offer spacious, heavily wooded resorts with 50-amp full hookups without the severe coastal traffic bottlenecks.

Positioned just 20 to 30 minutes from major regional attractions—including Mystic Seaport, Mystic Aquarium, Mohegan Sun, and Foxwoods Resort Casino—Bozrah offers a quiet woodland refuge after a day on the sound or at the venues.

🚚 Rig Clearance & Heavy Highway Routing Guidance

Navigating rural Connecticut in a 40+ foot Class A motorhome or heavy fifth wheel requires tactical route planning. Colonial-era stone bridges, low-clearance historic railway overpasses, and winding state two-lanes can trap unprepared drivers relying on standard automotive GPS apps.

Key Highway Approaches

  • From I-395 (North / South): I-395 is the primary interstate corridor for accessing Bozrah campgrounds. Exits 22 (CT-82) and 23 (CT-163) offer wide turning radiuses and smooth state-maintained access. Avoid commercial GPS shortcuts onto non-numbered township dirt connector roads.
  • From CT-2 (Eastbound from Hartford): Take Exit 23 onto CT-163 South directly into Bozrah. CT-2 provides generous overhead clearance, but watch for sudden grade transitions on off-ramps when hauling heavy fifth-wheel loads.
  • From I-95 (Coastal Route): When coming north from Rhode Island or NY, transition to I-395 North at Norwich. Avoid cutting across inland state routes like CT-117 or CT-214 with a large trailer due to tight, unbanked S-curves and overhanging oak canopies.
Pro Clearance Tip: Always keep your rig to the centerline when negotiating rural secondary roads like Lake Road or Scotland Road. Spring thaw conditions and soft shoulders along the Shetucket and Quinebaug River basins can pull heavy dual-rear-wheel axles toward drainage ditches.

⚡ Local Infrastructure & Utility Insights

  • Seasonal Power Grid Loads: During peak July and August summer weekends, regional grid draw can cause slight voltage drops in rural areas. We strongly recommend running a heavy-duty surge protector with auto-disconnect features to protect dual A/C units and residential refrigerators.
  • Water Pressure & Filtration: Many campgrounds in the Thames River watershed draw from deep private wells. Static pressure can spike above 70 PSI early in the morning—use an adjustable water pressure regulator set to 45–50 PSI and a dual-stage inline water filter.
  • Cellular Coverage: Verizon and AT&T deliver solid LTE and 5G coverage along the I-395 and CT-82 corridors, but signal strength drops in deep river valleys near Salt Rock and Hopeville Pond. A cellular signal booster is recommended if working remotely from your rig.
